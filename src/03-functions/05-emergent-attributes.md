
# Emergent Attributes

We briefly considered how functions inherit attributes from operations and how that plays out structurally. Before we name the emergent attributes, let's revisit the inherited attributes and reapply the two-column method we used to name attributes B and A^B, with the functions in mind.

## Attribute A

| A = 0                          | A = 1                            |
|--------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Insight**: Sense → Know      | **Impression**: Know → Predict   |
| **Impact**: Sense → Feel       | **Identification**: Know → Sense |
| **Emotion**: Predict → Feel    | **Calibration**: Feel → Sense    |
| **Conviction**: Predict → Know | **Opportunity**: Feel → Predict  |


What do Insight, Impact, Emotion, and Conviction have in common, and what separates them from Impression, Identification, Calibration, Opportunity? What is common between Impression, Identification, Calibration, and Opportunity?

A subject can hide Insight, Impact, Emotion, and Conviction by pure will or skill: an outside observer can't see them fully without crossing the subject's boundary. They are entirely internal. Bringing in Husserl's test, if you can't see the other side of it, it is internal. Conversely, for an outside observer, there's no other side of understanding (Insight), anger (Emotion), pain (Impact), or belief (Conviction).

Hiding an idea of Impression, Identification, Calibration, and Opportunity is much harder: a subject can't do that without a tool or an artifact. They are precisely external. Husserl's test: if you can see the other side of it in principle, it is external. Conversely, you can see a ball's color (Impression), identify its shape (Identification), notice whenever it is inflated (Calibration), or recognize its usefulness for a game (Opportunity) from any side.

| Internal (A = 0) | External (A = 1)   |
|------------------|--------------------|
| **Insight**      | **Impression**     |
| **Impact**       | **Identification** |
| **Emotion**      | **Calibration**    |
| **Conviction**   | **Opportunity**    |

## Attribute B

| B = 0                           | B = 1                            |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Calibration**: Feel → Sense   | **Impression**: Know → Predict   |
| **Opportunity**: Feel → Predict | **Identification**: Know → Sense |
| **Emotion**: Predict → Feel     | **Insight**: Sense → Know        |
| **Conviction**: Predict → Know  | **Impact**: Sense → Feel         |

Here's a similar pair of questions: What do Calibration, Opportunity, Emotion, and Conviction have in common, and what separates them from Impression, Identification, Insight, Impact? What do Impression, Identification, Insight, and Impact have in common?

Observer's internal predictions and feelings define
Calibration, Opportunity, Emotion, and Conviction. They are epistemologically subjective, arising from purely subjective internal factors. From the subject's perspective, they are how you relate to the world at large, an affordance. Without your expectations, you can't tell whether a tire is inflated (Calibration), whether a screwdriver is useful (Opportunity), whether someone is sad (Emotion), or whether someone deserves your trust (Conviction).

By comparison, Impression, Identification, Insight, and Impact do not require the subject's feelings or predictions; rather, they are epistemologically objective, stemming from external objective senses and signals. Or how the world relates to you, a substrate. No matter your expectations, the apple is red (Impression), a shape is a triangle (Identification), a key is in your hand (Insight), or the pain is severe (Impact).

| Subjective (B = 0) | Objective (B = 1)  |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Calibration**    | **Impression**     |
| **Opportunity**    | **Identification** |
| **Emotion**        | **Insight**        |
| **Conviction**     | **Impact**         |

## Attribute A^B

| A^B = 0                          | A^B = 1                         |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Impression**: Know → Predict   | **Calibration**: Feel → Sense   |
| **Identification**: Know → Sense | **Opportunity**: Feel → Predict |
| **Emotion**: Predict → Feel      | **Insight**: Sense → Know       |
| **Conviction**: Predict → Know   | **Impact**: Sense → Feel        |

What do Impression, Identification, Emotion, and Conviction have in common, and what separates them from Calibration, Opportunity, Insight, Impact? What do Calibration, Opportunity, Insight, and Impact have in common?

Observer's predictions or understandings are at the root of Impression, Identification, Emotion, and Conviction. They need no relation or reference; hence they are absolute: just as a red color is red by itself (Impression), a sphere is a sphere in the vacuum of space (Identification), an emotion can be felt just by reading about it (Emotion), and beliefs exist on their own (Conviction).

By comparison, Calibration, Opportunity, Insight, and Impact emerge in the observer's feelings and senses. They only make sense in relation to a reference; hence, relative: a conditioned room is especially cool when you were outside on a hot day (Calibration), a bottle of water is indispensable when you are thirsty (Opportunity), an old table wobbles when you shake it (Insight), and pain is gone after taking a painkiller if you were in pain before (Impact).

| Absolute (A^B = 0) | Relative (A^B = 1) |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Impression**     | **Calibration**    |
| **Identification** | **Opportunity**    |
| **Emotion**        | **Insight**        |
| **Conviction**     | **Impact**         |

So far, no surprises with the inherited attributes. For emergent attributes, we will use the very same approach: start with a pair of lists, ask what is common and what is different among members of either list, and see where it takes us.
